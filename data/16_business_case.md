# Business case and evaluation

> Fictional demonstration content. Not a real vendor claim.

The business case should begin with measurable workflow problems rather than an assumed return on investment. Examples: time spent locating approved revisions; hours required to compile handover registers; overdue change requests; and duplicated data entry across systems.

Suggested evaluation method: establish baseline with a small user group; run the same tasks with the pilot; record task completion time, accuracy, user confidence and unresolved issues; account for implementation and subscription costs; then assess whether expansion is justified.

Illustrative calculation only: annual net benefit = verified annual time savings valued at an agreed labor rate + other validated benefits − annual recurring costs − annualized implementation costs. No default percentage savings, payback period or ROI is asserted.

Executives should see strategic fit, risks, dependencies, governance and next-step decisions. The AI agent may build a qualitative business-case outline but must not fabricate customer results or benchmark statistics.
